How long does the tank take to drain?

A cylindrical tank with a small hole in its bottom. Water leaves through the hole, and the level falls. We want the time until the tank is empty.

The problem

A cylindrical tank has a base diameter of 1.8 m and a height of 2.45 m. The hole in the bottom has a diameter of 6 cm. How long does it take for all the water to flow out?

t = 0 sh = 2.45 mwater left: 100%

Notice that the level drops quickly at first and slowly at the end. The speed v depends on h, so a lower level pushes the water out more weakly. At 70× speed the whole 1050 s plays in 15 s.

The shaded strip is a very thin layer of water of thickness dh. The level h is the height of water above the hole.

What we are given

SymbolMeaningValue
Rradius of the tank (half of 1.8 m)0.9 m
rradius of the hole (half of 6 cm = 0.06 m)0.03 m
h0starting water height2.45 m
ggravitational acceleration10 m/s2
vspeed of the water leaving the hole0.6 √2gh
Tunknown: time until h = 0?

Step 1Write the idea as an equation

In a tiny time dt, the water that leaves the tank equals the water that passes through the hole.

Volume lost by the tank. The layer of water that disappears is a thin cylinder. Its base is the whole tank bottom, with area π R2, and its thickness is the drop in level, dh. The volume of any cylinder is base area × height:

Vtank = π R2 · (−dh)

Why is the volume through the hole π r2 · v · dt? Picture the water that leaves the hole during the short time dt as a thin column, like toothpaste squeezed out of a tube. The base of this column is the hole itself, a circle of radius r, so its area is π r2. The length of the column is how far the water travelled: speed × time = v · dt. And again, volume = base area × length, so we get π r2 · v · dt.

Vhole = π r2 · v · dt

The minus sign is there because h decreases, so dh is negative. Setting them equal:

π R2 (−dh) = π r2 v dt

Step 2Cancel π and insert the speed

Cancel π and replace v with 0.6 √(2gh):

R2 (−dh) = r2 · 0.6 √2gh · dt

Step 3Separate the variables

Put everything with h on the left and everything with t on the right. Divide both sides by R2 and by √h:

dh√h = −r2R2 · 0.6 √2g · dt

The factor 0.6 √(2g) does not depend on h, so it is just a constant.

Step 4Integrate both sides

The sign ∫ means “add up all the tiny pieces.” We add them from the start to the end. On the left side h goes from h0 down to 0. On the right side the time t goes from 0 to T. Both sides must cover the same process, so the limits match.

∫h00 dh√h = −r2R2 · 0.6 √2g ∫0T dt

Left side, part 1. Rewrite the root as a power, because powers are easy to integrate:

1√h = h−½

Left side, part 2. Power rule: to integrate hn, raise the power by 1, then divide by the new power. Here n = −½, so the new power is −½ + 1 = ½:

∫ h−½ dh = h½½ = 2h½ = 2√h

Quick check: differentiate the answer. (2h½)′ = 2 · ½ · h−½ = h−½, which is where we started.

Left side, part 3. Insert the limits: take the value at the top limit (0) and subtract the value at the bottom limit (h0):

[ 2√h ]h00 = 2√0 − 2√h0 = −2√h0

Right side. The part −r2R2 · 0.6 √(2g) has no h and no t, so it is a constant and can be moved outside the integral. What remains is ∫ dt, which equals t:

−r2R2 · 0.6 √2g · [ t ]0T = −r2R2 · 0.6 √2g · T

Both sides together:

−2√h0 = −r2R2 · 0.6 √2g · T

Cancel the minus signs on both sides:

2√h0 = r2R2 · 0.6 √2g · T

Step 5Solve for T

T = 2√h0 · R20.6 · r2 · √2g

To make the numbers easy, split this into three parts: a plain number, the ratio of the areas, and the roots:

T = 20.6 · R2r2 · √h0√2g

Here the roots change shape. A root divided by a root is the same number as one root over the whole fraction:

√h0√2g = √h02g

So the formula becomes:

T = 20.6 · R2r2 · √h02g

Step 6Plug in the numbers

First the ratio of the areas:

R2r2 = 0.920.032 = 302 = 900

Then the square root:

√2.452 · 10 = √0.1225 = 0.35

And the first factor:

20.6 = 3.33…

Put it all together:

T = 20.6 · 900 · 0.35 = 3000 · 0.35 = 1050 s

Answer

T = 1050 s

That is 17.5 minutes until the tank is completely empty.
